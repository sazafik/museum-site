# The Women Who Stood by the First Computers

> **Where we are on the timeline:** 1948–1960s. The previous article is about how MESM was built in Feofaniya (article 1). The next one is about how the computer went to the factory (article 3).

When you picture the people who created the first computers, who comes to mind? Most often, men in white shirts. But in Kyiv things were different: quite a few women stood by the first machines. Today we tell their story.

## The MESM team

A quick reminder: MESM was the first computer in the Soviet Union. It was built near Kyiv in 1948–1951 under the leadership of Sergey Lebedev. Back then such machines were called EOM — "electronic computing machines".

Among those who built and tuned MESM were I. Okulova, Z. Zorina-Rapota and L. Abalyshnikova. Their surnames have survived in the lists of participants on the website of the Virtual Museum of Computing in Ukraine. About many of them we know very little — often only a surname and the first letter of a first name.

Perhaps you know more? If your family has memories of these people, please share them with the museum.

## Kateryna Shkabara: the machine's "conductor"

Kateryna Shkabara worked on one of the most important parts of MESM — the control unit. Picture an orchestra: the musicians play, but someone has to show who comes in and when. The control unit is a kind of conductor inside the computer. It decides which step the machine takes next.

MESM was not her last project. She also joined the work on the next Kyiv computer — the "Kyiv" machine — where she worked on the so-called "group operation".

## A new machine, a new team

In 1954, on the initiative of the mathematician Boris Gnedenko, the lab was moved to the Institute of Mathematics. There, work began on the "Kyiv" computer. It was built by almost the same team that had built MESM.

Two people led the project: Lev Dashevsky and Kateryna Yushchenko. Think about it: in the mid-1950s, a woman became one of the leaders behind a large computer. Later, from 1956–1957, the work was headed by Viktor Glushkov.

## Kateryna Yushchenko: how to tell a machine what to do

Kateryna Yushchenko (born Rvachova) was born on 8 December 1919.

In 1955, together with V. Korolyuk, she created the Address Language. It is a language a person uses to "explain" to a machine what to do. It is considered one of the first languages of this kind in the world and the first in the USSR. We explain how it works and why it mattered in article 5.

Kateryna Yushchenko became the first woman in the USSR to earn the degree of Doctor of Physical and Mathematical Sciences for her work in programming. In other words, she proved to top scientists that "explaining" tasks to a machine is a serious science in its own right.

## Why this story matters

These women joined the new field when the word "programmer" did not even exist yet. The first machines existed in just one or two copies, so they had to learn on the job. And what they came up with back then later became a foundation for those who came after them.

## Fun fact

Kateryna Yushchenko's Address Language was used not only on the "Kyiv". It was also used on other computers of that time — including the "Ural", the "Minsk" and the "Dnipro".

## Bridge to today

Today girls sometimes hear that IT is "not for them". The story of Kyiv's computers shows that in Ukraine things started out very differently.

## Bridge to memories

Many people remember a mother, aunt or neighbour who worked "on the EOM" at a computing centre. Perhaps it is thanks to them that these machines worked every day.

## Talk about it at home

- Did you know there were so many women working on the first computers?
- Are there women in your family who worked with technology or in science? Ask them about it.
- What job would you change for something new and exciting?
