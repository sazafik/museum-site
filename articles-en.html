1948–1957

Hot as the Tropics: How the First Computer Was Built Near Kyiv

Imagine a room where six thousand glass tubes are glowing at the same time. It gets so hot that the ceiling has to be taken apart. That is how the first computer in the Soviet Union was born, just outside Kyiv. Back then, such machines were called EOM — the Ukrainian and Russian abbreviation for "electronic computing machine".

The Women Who Stood by the First Computers

When you picture the people who created the first computers, who comes to mind? Most often, men in white shirts. But in Kyiv things were different: quite a few women stood by the first machines. Today we tell their story.

ARTICLE_03.TXT

1958–1975

The Computer Goes to the Factory: How the "Dnipro" Learned to Run Machines

The first computers were a real wonder, but a wonder that existed in a single copy. Picture it: the machine fills a whole room, a team of scientists keeps watch beside it, and there is no second one like it. What if you made a computer that a factory could turn out by the hundreds? And one that would not just calculate, but also run other machines by itself? That is exactly what the "Dnipro" became.

A Computer for a Single Engineer: the "Promin" and the MIR

The first computers were huge, and whole teams of specialists worked with them. An ordinary engineer designing a bridge or a machine tool could hardly get near one. What if you built a computer that one person could work with alone? That is what people in Kyiv dreamed of in the 1960s.

How a Machine Was Taught to Understand People: the "Kyiv" Computer and the First Programming Languages

A computer is very fast, but on its own it knows nothing. Every task has to be explained to it step by step. How do you do that if the machine does not understand human language? In the 1950s people in Kyiv worked on this question too — and found one of the first answers in the world.
